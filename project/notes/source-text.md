ARGENTO SCIENTIFIC
Website Redesign
Vision & Strategy
A plan for modernizing Argento's web presence to better serve
its buyers and generate more qualified leads.

July 2026

The opportunity

Argento Scientific has built a strong reputation in downstream process development — one that travels almost entirely by word of mouth. Customers at major pharmaceutical and biopharmaceutical companies refer colleagues. Scientists switch jobs and bring their vendor preferences with them. This is a powerful commercial foundation.

The website has not kept pace with this reputation. It functions as a digital business card rather than a conversion tool — it confirms that Argento exists, but does not actively work to build trust, showcase technical depth, or move a visitor toward a quote request. For a company whose buyers arrive already curious and pre-disposed to engage, this is a significant missed opportunity.

The core insight
Because most buyers arrive via word-of-mouth, the website's primary job is trust confirmation — not cold discovery. It needs to quickly answer: "Is this vendor legitimate? Do serious teams like mine use this?" A modernized site doesn't need to shout. It needs to be precise, credible, and easy to act on.

BUYERS
Who we're designing for

Research into Argento's customer base — informed by LinkedIn data and job posting analysis — reveals two distinct buyer profiles for the chromatography columns line. Understanding who these people are, what they care about, and how they make decisions shapes every recommendation in this document.

Persona 1 · The Bench Scientist

Titles
Research Associate, Associate Scientist, Scientist I/II, Biologist I — Downstream Process Development
Experience
2–6 years. Early-to-mid career, executing on someone else's experimental plan.
Education
BS or MS in biochemistry, chemistry, or chemical engineering
Core motivation
Time savings. Column packing is necessary but pulls them from the science that actually advances their career.
How they find Argento
Word-of-mouth from a labmate or peer at another company. They arrive already curious.
Role in purchase
Internal champion — not the final buyer. Needs something to forward to procurement or a lab manager.
What they need from the site
Fast trust confirmation and forwardable content. Social proof from peers matters most.

Persona 2 · The Senior Technical Lead

Titles
Senior Principal Scientist, Lab Head, Senior Scientist — Purification PD or Process Development
Experience
10+ years, often PhD-level. Directs research; sets vendor strategy.
Education
PhD in Biochemistry, Molecular Biology, or related field; often multiple advanced degrees
Core motivation
Technical precision. Needs columns that match modeled parameters exactly, delivered fast.
Focus areas
Mechanistic modeling, digital twins, high-throughput experimentation, computational chromatography
How they find Argento
Peer networks, conferences, industry collaboration. May have direct purchasing authority.
What they need from the site
Technical depth — specific specs, resin range, proof of custom capability.

CURRENT STATE
Where the site falls short

A full UX assessment of argentoscientific.com — including a review of 14 product PDFs currently linked from the site — identified the following issues, ordered by impact.

Critical — fix immediately

1.  Hero copy misses both personas
"Simplify downstream process development" is accurate but abstract. It doesn't speak to the Bench Scientist's core pain (time lost to column packing) or the Senior Lead's priority (technical precision on demand). First-time visitors can't tell at a glance what Argento sells or why they should care.

2.  No social proof anywhere on the site
The homepage claims to serve "some of the biggest biopharmaceutical companies in the world" but names no customers, shows no logos, and includes no testimonials. Both buyer personas are driven by peer validation — it's how they found Argento in the first place. The absence of proof at the moment of confirmation is the single biggest trust gap on the site.

3.  Product spec table is an image, not HTML
The bed height/volume table in the Column Catalog — covering 7 column diameters and dozens of size/volume combinations — is embedded as a screenshot on the product page. This makes it invisible to search engines and inaccessible to screen readers. The Senior Technical Lead, who validates vendors by checking exact column parameters, cannot search for this data.

4.  Multiple PDF links are broken
The site links to product brochures and instruction sheets that return 404 errors. For a buyer using the site to confirm a colleague's recommendation — or to forward a link to their lab manager — a broken link at that moment ends the engagement. Additionally, several documents exist in multiple versions (three Solvent Sentry instruction sheets, three SeptaCaps catalogs, two VertiRack catalogs) with no clear indication of which is current.

High priority — significant conversion impact

Column Swap Program undersold
The program has a compelling, specific value proposition (1-week turnaround, FIFO resin traceability, lot certification, US-packed, diameters 3–50mm) — none of which is visible on the website. The PDF brochure content should become a dedicated landing page and homepage feature.
Resin list locked in PDF
A partial list of 30+ packed resins (Cytiva MabSelect variants, Capto Q, TMAE, Toyopearl, POROS AAV) is buried in the column catalog PDF — completely unsearchable. Moving it to an HTML table on the columns page would drive significant qualified search traffic from scientists looking for specific resin support.
Single CTA excludes Solvent Safety buyers
The only navigation CTA is "Request a column quote." Solvent Safety buyers — a distinct persona with different needs — have no clear entry point or call to action anywhere on the site.
No forwardable content for internal champions
The Bench Scientist doesn't make the final purchase decision — they advocate internally. The site currently offers a full quote form (high commitment) or broken PDF links. There is no shareable one-pager, spec sheet, or product overview suited to internal distribution.
Solvent Sentry™ product content not readable
The Solvent Sentry™ overview (float switch mechanism, 100ft wireless bridge, polypropylene construction, multi-alert system) exists only as a PDF with a broken link. There is likely minimal readable HTML content on the product page itself, making it effectively invisible to search engines.
No pricing signal
Without any ballpark pricing, the Bench Scientist's internal champion cannot make even a rough case to procurement before requesting a formal quote. The Column Swap Program's "only pay to pack after initial hardware purchase" framing is already a cost signal — it should be surfaced more prominently.

Medium priority — meaningful but lower urgency

No technical content for Senior Leads
The Senior Technical Lead expects technical depth: white papers, application notes, or detailed FAQs. The existing Digital Library is a start — but it's buried under "Company Info," the last place a researcher would look.
Full product range not visible
VertiRack™, SeptaCaps™, and the full solvent safety accessories line (emissions control, waste collection, closed systems) are not visible on the homepage. A Senior Lead managing a whole lab might buy across multiple product lines — but only if they know the range exists.
No secondary lead capture
The only conversion path is the full quote form. A visitor doing early-stage research before a project begins — not ready for a quote — has no way to stay connected. A content download (resin compatibility guide, column sizing reference) could capture these early-funnel visitors.
Platform limitations
Wix has meaningful constraints on technical SEO customization and page performance that will matter as the site scales. These limitations should be evaluated before investing heavily in the current build.

VISION
What we're building toward

The redesigned Argento Scientific website has one primary job: to take a visitor who arrived via a colleague's recommendation and quickly give them everything they need to trust Argento, understand what they're buying, and take the next step — whether that's requesting a quote, forwarding a link internally, or downloading a spec sheet.

It is not a mass-market website. It does not need to rank for broad awareness keywords or attract cold traffic. Its value comes from being precise, credible, and easy to act on for a technically sophisticated buyer who already has context.

Design principles

Trust first
Every page should answer "is this vendor legit?" before asking for anything. Social proof, technical specificity, and clear credentials come before CTAs.
Speak to the scientist
Copy should be written for a PhD-level audience that will immediately detect vague or generic language. Precise, concrete, and outcome-focused — not marketing-speak.
Make it forwardable
The Bench Scientist is an internal champion. Every key page should be something they're comfortable sending to a lab manager or procurement contact. Clean, professional, and link-stable.
Specs belong on the web
Any data a scientist would use to evaluate or specify a product — column dimensions, resin list, pressure ratings — belongs in readable HTML, not locked in a PDF or embedded as an image.
Two product lines, two entry points
Columns buyers and Solvent Safety buyers have different needs, different job titles, and different decision paths. The site should make it immediately clear which path belongs to whom.
Earn the quote request
The quote form is not the first impression — it's the destination. The content before it should do enough work that by the time a visitor reaches the form, they already know what they want.

Navigation structure

The redesigned nav uses three top-level items: Chromatography columns (dropdown containing: Columns overview, Column Swap Program, Spec table), Solvent Safety (dropdown containing: Solvent Sentry™, Safety systems — pending persona), and About. Two CTAs sit in the nav on all pages: a primary blue button ("Request a column quote") and a secondary teal ghost button ("Learn about Solvent Sentry™"). Resources has been deliberately removed from the nav at launch. The existing Digital Library is a list of broken PDF links — adding a Resources nav item pointing to broken content would undermine credibility with the Senior Technical Lead. Once the PDF library is audited, fixed, and confirmed live, a Resources slot can be added in a later phase. Product-level PDFs (installation instructions, spec sheets, one-pagers) link directly from their product pages rather than from a central library.

Immediate orientation: within 3 seconds, a visitor knows what Argento sells, who it's for, and what makes it worth contacting.
Trust confirmation: customer logos or credibility signals appear before the fold, answering the word-of-mouth buyer's first question.
Dual entry points: two nav dropdowns (Chromatography columns, Solvent Safety) and two CTAs (primary column quote button, secondary Solvent Sentry ghost button), so neither product line feels like an afterthought.
Column Swap Program prominence: featured on the homepage as a differentiated offer with a clear "how it works" summary, not buried in a dropdown.

What success looks like
A downstream process scientist hears about Argento from a colleague at a conference. They visit the site that evening on their phone. Within 60 seconds they've seen that serious teams use Argento, confirmed their resin is supported, understood the Column Swap Program, and sent the link to their lab manager. They request a quote the next morning. That is the conversion this site is designed to enable.

Trust confirmation, not discovery.
